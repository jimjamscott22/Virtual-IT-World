Most tickets are yours. A few are not, and clearing the symptom on one of
those is worse than leaving it open — it destroys the evidence, or commits the
company to something you were not asked to decide.

## Check

Before you touch anything, ask which of these the ticket is:

1. **Does fixing it need somebody's authorisation?** Restoring access that was
   deliberately taken away is a decision with an owner, and that owner is not
   the service desk.
2. **Is the failing part physical?** No command repairs hardware. Confirm the
   diagnosis, then hand it over with the reading that shows it.
3. **Would acting destroy the evidence?** If the interesting thing about the
   ticket is *when and how* something was changed, removing the change removes
   the answer along with it.
4. **Do you actually know the correct value?** Some fields have one right
   answer and it lives in a record you do not hold. Guessing at it is not a
   fix, it is a second ticket.

If none of the four applies, it is yours. Work it.

## Notes

Escalating well is a skill with its own standard: name the reading you took,
quote what it said, and say plainly which of the four reasons above puts the
ticket outside your scope. A handoff that says only "please fix" comes back,
and it comes back slower than doing the work yourself would have been.
